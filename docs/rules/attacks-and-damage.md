### 1. Official rule

#### 1.1 The attack roll, and rolling 1 or 20 (crit trigger)

Identical mechanic in both editions. **2014** `combat.md` lines 250, 262–266: "To make an attack roll, roll a d20 and add the appropriate modifiers. If the total of the roll plus modifiers equals or exceeds the target's Armor Class (AC), the attack hits... If the d20 roll for an attack is a 20, the attack hits regardless of any modifiers or the target's AC. This is called a critical hit... If the d20 roll for an attack is a 1, the attack misses regardless of any modifiers or the target's AC." **2024** `combat.md` (Rolling 1/20 equivalent under d20 Tests) states the same "natural 20 always hits / natural 1 always misses" rule with no differing wording found in this grounding set.

**Verdict: the only thing that triggers a critical hit in the base rules is a natural 20 on the attack roll's d20.** Nothing else in either edition's grounding text triggers one (no "beat AC by 5" or similar house-rule variant is described as SRD-default in this grounding set — checked `combat.md` in both editions end to end for any other crit-trigger language, found none).

#### 1.2 Critical hit damage — dice doubled, not modifiers

**2014** `combat.md` lines 375–379 (`### Critical Hits`): "When you score a critical hit, you get to roll extra dice for the attack's damage against the target. **Roll all of the attack's damage dice twice and add them together. Then add any relevant modifiers as normal.** ... For example, if you score a critical hit with a dagger, roll 2d4 for the damage, rather than 1d4, and then add your relevant ability modifier. If the attack involves other damage dice, such as from the rogue's Sneak Attack feature, you roll those dice twice as well."

**2024** `combat.md` line 133 (`Critical Hits`): "roll all the attack's damage dice twice, add modifiers once."

**Confirmed exactly as suspected: the dice are doubled (rolled twice, or equivalently doubled dice-count), the flat modifier (ability modifier, magic weapon bonus, etc.) is added only once.** Both editions state this identically in substance; 2024's wording is simply terser. This also confirms extra damage-dice sources bolted onto the same attack (Sneak Attack, a Smite, a bonus damage die from a feature) double along with the base weapon/spell dice — 2014 says so explicitly ("you roll those dice twice as well"); 2024's grounding text doesn't restate the multi-source case but nothing in it narrows the rule to only the base weapon dice, and the general "all the attack's damage dice" phrasing covers it.

#### 1.3 Order of operations: crit doubling vs. resistance/vulnerability — confirmed, doubling happens BEFORE

Neither edition's grounding text states the crit/resistance interaction as a single explicit combined sentence, but the order is fully determined by chaining two rules each edition **does** state explicitly, not by inference beyond that chain:

- **2014** `combat.md` line 419 (`### Damage Resistance and Vulnerability`): "**Resistance and then vulnerability are applied after all other modifiers to damage.**" A critical hit's extra dice are rolled and summed as part of computing "the damage" (§1.2 — you roll the doubled dice, add modifiers, that *is* the damage roll) — they are not a "modifier to damage" applied afterward, they are inputs to the damage total itself. Resistance/vulnerability's "after all other modifiers" therefore means after the crit-doubled total is already computed.
- **2024** `combat.md` lines 138–141: "Order of application: **flat adjustments first, then Resistance, then Vulnerability.**" Same structure: the raw damage total (crit-doubled dice + modifiers, already a finished number by this point) is the input flat adjustments/resistance/vulnerability operate on.

**Verdict: critical-hit dice doubling happens first (it's part of computing the raw damage number), and resistance/vulnerability/immunity are applied to that already-doubled total afterward, never the other way around.** This is the correct, common-bug-avoiding order: a monster resistant to slashing hit for a critical 2d6+3 slashing (say the dice come up 12) takes `(12 + 3) / 2 = 7` (round down per §1.5), not `(6 + 3) rolled-then-doubled` or any order that halves before doubling the dice. Both editions' grounding text is explicit enough on ordering (2014's "after all other modifiers," 2024's numbered list) that this is a citation-backed conclusion, not a guess — but note neither text uses the word "critical" in the same sentence as "resistance," so the chain is the confirmed mechanism, flagged here so it isn't mistaken for a single direct quote.

#### 1.4 Resistance / vulnerability / immunity — math and stacking

**2014** `combat.md` lines 413–421 (`### Damage Resistance and Vulnerability`): "If a creature or an object has **resistance** to a damage type, damage of that type is **halved** against it. If a creature or an object has **vulnerability** to a damage type, damage of that type is **doubled** against it... Multiple instances of resistance or vulnerability that affect the same damage type count as only one instance. For example, if a creature has resistance to fire damage as well as resistance to all nonmagical damage, the damage of a nonmagical fire is reduced by half against the creature, not reduced by three-quarters." **No explicit rounding direction is stated for the halving in 2014's grounding text** — checked the full section, "halved" is used without a "round down"/"round up" qualifier anywhere in this file.

**2024** `combat.md` lines 138–142: "**Resistance** halves damage of that type (**round down**); **Vulnerability** doubles it. Multiple instances of the same Resistance/Vulnerability don't stack. Order of application: flat adjustments first, then Resistance, then Vulnerability. **Immunity** to a damage type/condition means no effect from it at all." **2024 explicitly states "round down"** where 2014 is silent.

**Verdict:**
- Resistance = half (round down, per 2024's explicit text; 2014 doesn't contradict it and 5e's universal round-down convention for halved values — used identically for the "half damage on a successful save" rule below and for `standUpCostFt` in `docs/rules/movement.md` §1.6 — makes round-down the correct read for 2014 too, flagged as extension-by-consistency for 2014 specifically since its own text doesn't say so).
- Vulnerability = double, flat, no rounding question (doubling an integer never produces a fraction).
- Immunity = the damage type has zero effect, full stop (2024's explicit wording; 2014's grounding text in this section doesn't use the word "immunity" at all in this passage, but the concept is confirmed elsewhere in the skill's monster-facing convention and this app's own existing `damage_immunities` column, which predates this rule doc).
- **Multiple sources of resistance to the same type do not stack** (explicit both editions: "count as only one instance" / "don't stack") — a creature resistant to fire from two different sources still only halves once, not quarters.
- **Both resistance AND vulnerability to the same damage type simultaneously**: neither edition's grounding text states this combination's outcome directly — it's not named as a case in either "Damage Resistance and Vulnerability" section. **This is a confirmed gap, not filled with invented specificity.** The 2024 text's explicit ordering ("flat adjustments first, then Resistance, then Vulnerability") is the only lever available, and applying it literally in the stated order (halve first, then double the halved result) returns the original, unmodified value for any even damage total and rounds in the resistance's favor by 1 for odd totals (e.g. raw 25: resist→12 [round down], then vulnerable→24, one lower than the raw 25) — but this is **this doc's inference from the stated order-of-operations sentence, not a directly confirmed ruling for the both-at-once case**, and it's also the well-known published 5e designer guidance (not present in this grounding set) that resistance and vulnerability to the same type on the same creature are intended to cancel out. Flagging explicitly: don't cite this doc as SRD-confirmed for the both-at-once case beyond "apply resistance then vulnerability in that order, per the stated general ordering rule" — a DM who deliberately grants both (the SRD text calls this case "rare" nowhere directly, but it only arises from unusual DM-authored combinations, e.g. homebrew or unusual magic items) should be told the app computes it via literal order-of-operations, not a hardcoded "they cancel" special case, since no SRD text asserts a special-case cancellation rule to hardcode.
- **Per-hit or pooled?** Both editions' grounding text frames resistance/vulnerability entirely in terms of "damage of that type" from a single instance/example (a single attack's 25 bludgeoning damage, a single nonmagical fire instance) — there is no sentence in either edition's grounding text describing pooling multiple separate damage instances (e.g. two different attacks in the same turn) before applying resistance once. **Verdict: apply per-instance (per hit / per damage roll), not pooled across a turn** — this is the natural reading of every worked example in both editions (each example is a single damage-dealing event), and nothing suggests batching; flagged as the confirmed reading of "damage of that type" always referring to one damage roll's worth, not a stated prohibition on pooling (no edition text says "you must not pool damage before applying resistance" in so many words, but every example computes resistance against one roll's total, never a summed total from multiple attacks).

#### 1.5 Advantage/disadvantage on attack rolls, and crit determination with advantage

**2014** `ability-checks.md` lines 29–37 (`## Advantage and Disadvantage`): "Sometimes a special ability or spell tells you that you have advantage or disadvantage on an ability check, **a saving throw, or an attack roll**. When that happens, you roll a second d20 when you make the roll. **Use the higher of the two rolls if you have advantage, and use the lower roll if you have disadvantage.**" Explicitly includes attack rolls in the same general mechanic already confirmed for the base d20 system in `docs/rules/movement.md`'s sibling docs — no attack-roll-specific variant exists.

**2024** `ability-checks.md` lines 80–85 (`## Advantage / Disadvantage`): "Roll two d20s: use the **higher** with Advantage, the **lower** with Disadvantage. Multiple sources of Advantage (or of Disadvantage) don't stack — you still roll only two dice. If a roll has both Advantage and Disadvantage, they cancel out and you roll one d20 normally." Same mechanic, and line 10–13 confirms the general d20-test framing (including attack rolls) explicitly uses this same rule.

**Verdict: identical mechanic for attack rolls specifically as the general d20 rule — roll 2d20, keep higher (advantage) or lower (disadvantage), use only the kept die's value for everything downstream.**

**Crit determination with advantage/disadvantage**: neither edition's grounding text has a sentence that says "if you roll advantage and one of the two dice is a natural 20, treat it as a crit regardless of which die is kept." **This isn't a gap, though — it's answered by combining two rules each edition explicitly states, not invented:** (a) advantage/disadvantage's own text says you "use" only one of the two rolled numbers — the other is not "the roll," it simply isn't used for anything ("use the higher of the two rolls... use the lower roll" — 2014; "use the number from only one of them" — 2024's ability-checks.md line 12); (b) a crit is triggered by "the d20 roll for an attack" being a 20 (§1.1) — i.e., the value that *is* the attack roll, which under advantage/disadvantage is by definition the kept die, not the discarded one. **Verdict: a 20 only triggers a critical hit if it's the KEPT die.** Rolling advantage and getting a 20 and a 15 → the 20 is kept (higher) → critical hit, exactly as expected. Rolling **disadvantage** and getting a 20 and a 15 → the **lower** die (15) is kept, the 20 is discarded → **not a critical hit**, even though a natural 20 physically appeared on the table — the discarded die never mattered, its value (even 20, even 1) has zero mechanical effect under either edition's stated "use only one of them" text. This is a direct logical consequence of two explicitly-quoted rules chained together, not a guess, but flagged as a chained conclusion rather than a single directly-quoted sentence, per this doc's own citation discipline.

#### 1.6 Attack-roll attacks vs. save-based attacks, and crits on saves

Both shapes are real and SRD-grounded, not an app invention:
- **Attack-roll attacks** (weapon attacks, most monster melee/ranged actions, spell attacks like *fire bolt*): §1.1 above — roll d20 + modifier vs. target AC.
- **Save-based effects** (many spells — *fireball*, *fear* — and some monster/legendary actions): **2014** `ability-checks.md` lines 370–382 (`## Saving Throws`): "A saving throw... represents an attempt to resist a spell, a trap, a poison, a disease, or a similar threat... To make a saving throw, roll a d20 and add the appropriate ability modifier... The Difficulty Class for a saving throw is determined by the effect that causes it. **For example, the DC for a saving throw allowed by a spell is determined by the caster's spellcasting ability and proficiency bonus.**" This is a structurally different roll: the **target** rolls (not the attacker), against a **DC** the effect's source sets (not the attacker's to-hit total vs. the target's AC), and the roll's own mechanic never mentions "hit"/"miss"/AC/critical anywhere in this section. **2024**'s equivalent `## Saving Throws` section (line 51+) — not fully quoted here since its mechanic (d20 + ability modifier vs. a stated DC, success/failure) matches 2014's substance with no wording difference found on the roll mechanic itself.

**Can a save-based effect crit? No — confirmed by the *absence* of the mechanism, not by a sentence that says "saves cannot crit."** A critical hit is specifically and only defined in terms of the **attack roll's** d20 result reaching AC-independent-hit status on a natural 20 (§1.1) — a mechanic that exists only for attack rolls. A saving throw's d20 result determines the *target's* success/failure against a DC; nothing in either edition's Saving Throws section defines a "natural 20 on a save = double damage" or any analogous critical-success-on-a-save damage rule. **Grounding checked**: full text of both editions' Saving Throws sections, and both Critical Hits sections (§1.2, which only ever reference "the attack" and "the attack roll," never "a save" or "the target's saving throw roll"). No SRD text anywhere in this grounding set attaches critical-hit mechanics to a save-based effect. This confirms the assumption in the task brief — save-based damage always rolls its stated dice once (or per its own stated effect, e.g. "half damage on a success," §1.7 below), with no crit-doubling path, ever.

#### 1.7 Half damage on a successful save

**2014**: implied by individual spell/effect text (not itself in this grounding set's combat.md as a general rule — the 2014 grounding text doesn't have a standalone "half damage on save" general-rule sentence; it's per-effect wording in spell descriptions, which are catalog data, not in this rules-skill's scope per this agent's own "stay in your lane" constraint). **2024** `combat.md` line 137: "**Half damage** on a successful save is rounded down." This is stated as a general rule in 2024's grounding text specifically (not per-spell), confirming the round-down convention explicitly for this case too — consistent with §1.4's resistance rounding and `docs/rules/movement.md`'s prone stand-up rounding, the same universal 5e round-down convention recurring a third time in this app's rule set.

#### 1.8 The exhaustive damage-type list

`python3 .opencode/skills/dnd5e-srd/scripts/query.py 2014 damage-types --list` and the 2024 equivalent both return the **identical 13 types**: `acid, bludgeoning, cold, fire, force, lightning, necrotic, piercing, poison, psychic, radiant, slashing, thunder`. **2014** `combat.md` lines 381–411 and **2024** `combat.md` lines 381+ both enumerate the same 13 with matching example flavor text (bludgeoning = hammers/falling/constriction, etc., verified matching wording pattern in both files). **Verdict: no edition adds or removes a damage type — the list is exhaustive and identical in both editions**, confirmed by both the structured category data and the prose enumeration cross-checking each other.

### 2. Data model translation

#### 2.1 Characters need a new structured attacks list — the weapon-derived approach is NOT SRD-sufficient on its own

The task's own framing already identifies the real gap correctly, and the SRD grounding above confirms why the weapon-derived approach (`InventoryPanel.tsx`'s `weaponAttackAbilityModifier`) can't just be patched with a resistance layer and called done: a weapon-derived roll only ever knows about **equipped WEAPON items**. It has no representation for:
- Unarmed strikes (2014/2024 both name this as a real, always-available attack — `combat.md` line 300 in 2024: "you can use an **unarmed strike**... deals bludgeoning damage equal to 1 + your Strength modifier").
- Class features that are attacks but aren't weapon items (a monk's Martial Arts die, a warlock's Eldritch Blast cantrip, a paladin's Divine Smite as bonus dice on an existing attack).
- Spell attacks at all — `InventoryPanel.tsx`'s derivation is inventory-scoped by construction; a character's spell attacks (per `character_spells`, confirmed to exist as a separate table from this session's earlier `characters.ts` route reads) have no attack-roll/damage-roll trigger anywhere in the web app today, the same gap MonsterInstanceAttacks fills for monster actions but nothing fills for characters.
- Save-based character attacks (a spell like *burning hands* that targets a save, not an attack roll) — the weapon-item shape has no `saveDc`/`saveAbilityIndex` equivalent at all, and correctly so (it's the wrong shape for a weapon), but that means **no existing character-side code path can represent a save-based PC attack today**, structured or not.

**Verdict: characters need a new structured attacks list, directly analogous to monsters' existing `statBlockEntrySchema` shape, not an extension of the weapon-derivation helper.** Recommended: a `character_attacks` table (real rows, not JSONB) — unlike monster actions (rarely added/removed mid-campaign, JSONB-appropriate per this app's existing catalog-JSONB precedent), a PC's attack list changes moderately often in play (leveling up adds an attack, a feat swaps an unarmed strike's damage die, a temporary buff grants a new option) and — critically — **each row needs to be independently referenced by the new damage-application endpoint in §2.3 below** (an audit trail of "this HP change came from attack row N" is only possible if attacks are addressable rows, not opaque JSONB array entries with no stable id). This also matches the project's own catalog/instance-table precedent (`.opencode/agents/rpg-data-model-architect`'s catalog-vs-instance rule): a character's attack list is instance data (belongs to one specific PC, mutates with that PC's build), structurally the same tier as `character_spells`/`character_inventory`, not campaign-shared catalog data — so a real table, not a JSONB blob on `characters`, is the right shape by this app's own established convention (contrast monster *actions*, which are catalog-scoped JSONB precisely because they describe the shared archetype, not a specific instance).

```sql
CREATE TABLE character_attacks (
  id                  BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
  character_id        BIGINT NOT NULL REFERENCES characters(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  name                TEXT NOT NULL,               -- "Unarmed Strike", "Longsword", "Eldritch Blast", "Burning Hands"
  source_item_id       BIGINT REFERENCES character_inventory(id) ON DELETE SET NULL,
    -- nullable: weapon-derived attacks (existing InventoryPanel case) link back
    -- to the inventory row so damage/proficiency stays derived from the item's
    -- own catalog entry where that's still correct (e.g. magic weapon bonus);
    -- NULL for class-feature/spell/unarmed attacks with no backing item.
  attack_bonus        INT,          -- NULL when save-based (mutually exclusive with save_dc, see CHECK below)
  damage_dice         TEXT,         -- e.g. "1d8", nullable for a pure-effect action with no damage roll
  damage_type         TEXT,         -- FK-by-name into the damage-types catalog (§1.8's 13, both editions identical)
  save_dc             INT,          -- NULL when attack-roll-based
  save_ability_index  TEXT,         -- e.g. "dex" — only meaningful alongside save_dc
  half_on_save        BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT true,  -- §1.7 — most save effects halve, but not all (e.g. some are all-or-nothing); DM/player-editable per attack row rather than hardcoded true
  notes                TEXT,
  sort_order           INT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  CHECK (
    (attack_bonus IS NOT NULL AND save_dc IS NULL)
    OR (attack_bonus IS NULL AND save_dc IS NOT NULL)
    OR (attack_bonus IS NULL AND save_dc IS NULL)   -- a pure-flavor action with neither, allowed (mirrors monsters' statBlockEntrySchema, which makes both optional today)
  )
);
CREATE INDEX ON character_attacks (character_id, sort_order);
```
This shape is deliberately close to `statBlockEntrySchema`'s field names (`attackBonus`/`damageDice`/`damageType`/`saveDc`/`saveAbilityIndex`) so the web layer's rendering logic (already built once for `MonsterInstanceAttacks`) generalizes to a shared `<AttackList>` component reading either monster JSONB actions or character `character_attacks` rows through one shared TS interface, rather than staying two parallel, drifting implementations.

#### 2.2 `computeAppliedDamage` — pure function, and exactly what it computes

New pure function, `packages/server/src/services/damage.ts` (new file — `services/hp.ts` stays focused on the temp-HP-absorption math per its existing single-responsibility scope; this is a distinct, composable step that runs *before* `applyHpDeltaWithTempAbsorption`, not a replacement for it), matching this codebase's established pure-function-first precedent (`services/hp.ts`, `services/movement.ts`):

```ts
export interface DamageTarget {
  resistances: string[];   // damage-type names, lowercase, matching the 13-type catalog (§1.8)
  vulnerabilities: string[];
  immunities: string[];
}

export interface RawDamageInput {
  diceExpression: string;   // e.g. "1d8", already-rolled server-side by diceRolls.ts — see §2.3, this function does NOT roll dice itself
  rolledDiceTotal: number;  // the SUM of the dice portion only (not the modifier) — needed separately because critical doubling (§1.2) only re-rolls/doubles the dice, never the modifier
  modifier: number;
  damageType: string | null;   // null = typeless damage (rare, e.g. some "force" effects that are pre-typed elsewhere, or untyped damage from a DM-authored homebrew effect) — untyped damage is never resisted/vulnerable/immune, per the general rule that resistance/vulnerability/immunity are always keyed to a named type
  isCritical: boolean;    // caller determines this from the KEPT d20 per §1.5 — this function does not see the d20 roll at all, only the already-doubled-or-not dice total
}

export interface AppliedDamageResult {
  rawTotal: number;         // dice + modifier, pre-resistance (post-crit-doubling, since doubling already happened before this function is called — see below)
  appliedDamage: number;    // final, post resistance/vulnerability/immunity — this is what feeds applyHpDeltaWithTempAbsorption as the (negative) delta
  breakdown: {
    diceTotal: number;
    modifier: number;
    resistanceApplied: boolean;
    vulnerabilityApplied: boolean;
    immune: boolean;
  };
}

export function computeAppliedDamage(raw: RawDamageInput, target: DamageTarget): AppliedDamageResult;
```

**Where critical-hit doubling happens, precisely**: per §1.2/§1.3, doubling is a **dice-rolling-time** concern (roll the dice twice / double the dice count), not a post-roll arithmetic step — so `computeAppliedDamage` itself does **not** double anything; it receives `rolledDiceTotal` **already reflecting the doubled dice** (the caller — the new endpoint in §2.3 — is responsible for asking `diceRolls.ts`'s roller for double the dice count when `isCritical` is true, exactly mirroring how a human at the table physically picks up 2d8 instead of 1d8 for a crit). `isCritical` is still passed into `computeAppliedDamage` for two reasons that remain relevant post-roll: (a) `breakdown` should be able to report "this was a critical hit" for the client/audit log without the caller separately threading that fact through two functions, and (b) it's needed to correctly implement the "damage at 0 HP" death-save-failure-count rule (§1.2/2014 line 453, §2024 line 156: 2 failures instead of 1 on a critical hit at 0 HP) — but that's `services/hp.ts`'s concern (death saves), not `computeAppliedDamage`'s; flagging so the field doesn't look unused inside this function's own math, which is exactly the resistance/vulnerability/immunity ordering from §1.3/§1.4, applied once to the pre-computed `rawTotal = rolledDiceTotal + modifier`:

```ts
function computeAppliedDamage(raw, target) {
  const rawTotal = raw.rolledDiceTotal + raw.modifier;
  if (raw.damageType === null) return { rawTotal, appliedDamage: rawTotal, breakdown: { ...no type-based effects... } };
  const type = raw.damageType.toLowerCase();
  if (target.immunities.includes(type)) return { rawTotal, appliedDamage: 0, breakdown: { immune: true, ... } };
  let applied = rawTotal;
  if (target.resistances.includes(type)) applied = Math.floor(applied / 2);   // §1.4, round down
  if (target.vulnerabilities.includes(type)) applied = applied * 2;           // §1.4 — order: resistance THEN vulnerability, per both editions' explicit ordering text
  return { rawTotal, appliedDamage: applied, breakdown: { resistanceApplied: ..., vulnerabilityApplied: ..., immune: false } };
}
```
This directly implements §1.4's confirmed both-at-once behavior (resistance applied first, vulnerability applied to the already-halved result) via the literal stated order, per §1.4's own flagged caveat that this specific combination isn't independently confirmed by a dedicated sentence in either edition — the ordering IS confirmed, the combination's net effect is this doc's traced consequence of that ordering, not a separately-cited rule.

#### 2.3 Plugging into the HP endpoints — yes, a new sibling endpoint is needed

**Confirmed: the existing signed-delta endpoints (`PATCH /characters/:id/hp`, `PATCH /monster-instances/:id/hp`) cannot host this** — they take a pre-computed `{delta, tempDelta}` from the client by design (that's the entire shape of `hpDeltaSchema`/`monsterInstanceHpDeltaSchema` per the routes read above), and resistance/vulnerability math needs the **pre-resistance raw damage and its type** server-side to compute anything — a client sending a final post-resistance number is exactly the trust boundary this task is trying to close (this app's own `services/diceRolls.ts` comment states the precedent plainly: "the RNG lives here and ONLY here... closing the obvious cheating vector where a player-supplied result could just be lied about" — the same principle applies to a player-supplied *already-resisted* damage number, which would let a malicious or buggy client simply not apply a monster's resistance at all).

**Recommended new endpoint**: `POST /characters/:id/apply-damage` and `POST /monster-instances/:id/apply-damage` (siblings to the existing `PATCH .../hp`, not a replacement — heal/temp-HP grants with no damage-type/resistance question still go through the existing simple delta endpoint unchanged), body:
```ts
{ diceExpression: string; damageType: string | null; isCritical: boolean; rollContext?: string }
```
Server-side flow, inside one transaction (same row-locking discipline `docs/rules/movement.md` §2.4 flags as needed for `setParticipantPosition` — this endpoint has the identical "read current state, compute, write" race shape and needs the same `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` treatment `applyActionEconomy` already uses):
1. Authorize exactly as `PATCH .../hp` does today (DM or the character's own owner — reuse `authorizeCharacterMutation`/DM-only-for-monster-instances, unchanged).
2. Roll the dice **server-side** via the same primitive `diceRolls.ts` already uses (`rollDie`) — dice count doubled when `isCritical` is true (§2.2's "caller doubles before calling `computeAppliedDamage`"), producing `rolledDiceTotal`. This keeps the "RNG lives here and only here" invariant intact for damage rolls exactly as it already holds for attack-roll d20s — today's gap is that `diceRolls.ts`'s `rollDice` DOES already roll damage dice server-side (`roll_type` supports arbitrary NdM per its own comment) but nothing downstream ever *applies* that rolled total to HP with resistance math; this endpoint is the missing consumer, not a new roller.
3. Load the target's `resistances`/`vulnerabilities`/`immunities` (§2.4 below — new columns needed for characters) and call `computeAppliedDamage`.
4. Feed `appliedDamage` (negated, since it's damage) as `delta` into the existing `applyHpDeltaWithTempAbsorption` — **reused unchanged**, since temp-HP-absorption math is orthogonal to resistance math and already correct.
5. Return both the dice-roll record (for the client's roll-log display, matching `MonsterInstanceAttacks`'s existing UX of showing what was rolled) and the `AppliedDamageResult` breakdown (so a DM/player can see "12 raw → 6 applied, resisted" rather than a silent number change) — the same encounter-sync broadcast (`broadcastHpChanged`) fires exactly as it does today, unchanged.

This is additive, not a breaking change to the existing simple-delta endpoints — healing, temp-HP grants, and any DM manual HP correction (typo fixes, homebrew effects with no dice/type) keep using the existing `PATCH .../hp` exactly as today.

#### 2.4 Characters need `damage_vulnerabilities`/`damage_resistances`/`damage_immunities` columns — confirmed real gap, not skippable

The task is right to flag this and right that it shouldn't be skipped: SRD-real PC/NPC resistance sources exist in both editions (2014 Dwarf poison resistance is a named racial trait; a Barbarian's Rage grants resistance to bludgeoning/piercing/slashing while raging in both editions) and this app's `characters` table currently has **zero** columns for any of the three (confirmed by reading `1784269737666_create-characters.ts` in full — no `damage_*` columns exist, unlike `monsters`' three `TEXT[]` columns from `1784269736666_create-catalog-monsters.ts`).

Two distinct sources of a character's resistance need separate handling, and conflating them would be a real bug:
- **Permanent/build-derived resistance** (a Dwarf's poison resistance, a Tiefling's fire resistance depending on subrace/edition) — this is effectively static for the character's whole existence, analogous to monsters' catalog-level columns. Add matching columns directly on `characters`:
```sql
ALTER TABLE characters ADD COLUMN damage_resistances TEXT[] NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}';
ALTER TABLE characters ADD COLUMN damage_vulnerabilities TEXT[] NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}';
ALTER TABLE characters ADD COLUMN damage_immunities TEXT[] NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}';
```
- **Temporary/conditional resistance** (Rage's resistance, which only applies while raging and disappears when Rage ends) — this is **not** the same tier as the columns above; this app already has a mechanism for exactly this kind of temporary, toggle-on-toggle-off state: the `active_effects` table (referenced in `docs/rules/movement.md` §2.4's "Incapacitated" gap discussion as this app's existing conditions/effects instance table). **Recommend**: an active effect definition can itself carry a `grants_resistance: string[]` (or equivalent) field, and `computeAppliedDamage`'s caller (the new endpoint in §2.3) unions `characters.damage_resistances` with any currently-active effect's granted resistances before calling `computeAppliedDamage` — **not** by writing Rage's resistance into the permanent columns and remembering to remove it, which is exactly the kind of client-trusted, easy-to-forget-to-revert state this app's broader "server-authoritative" convention exists to avoid. This union-at-read-time approach also means computeAppliedDamage itself never needs to know about the temporary/permanent distinction — its `DamageTarget` input is just three flat string arrays, already unioned by the caller, keeping the pure function pure and simple.

### 3. Edge cases

1. **Untyped/typeless damage** (§2.2) — a `damageType: null` input must never be checked against resistance/vulnerability/immunity arrays (there's no type to match); `computeAppliedDamage` must short-circuit this case explicitly, not silently no-op-match against an empty string.
2. **Immunity beats vulnerability regardless of order authored** — if a target is somehow both immune and vulnerable to the same type (DM-authored oddity, e.g. copy-paste error in a homebrew monster's arrays), immunity must win (apply zero damage) rather than the vulnerability check running first and doubling zero into zero-but-via-the-wrong-code-path — not itself a stated SRD sentence, but the only reading consistent with "immunity means no effect from it at all" (2024, quoted §1.4) being unconditional, not "no effect unless also vulnerable."
3. **Resistance AND vulnerability to the same type together** (§1.4) — confirmed-by-inference-from-ordering only, not independently SRD-stated; implement via the literal resistance-then-vulnerability order, and comment the code pointing at this doc section rather than presenting it as a separately-cited rule.
4. **Critical hit on a multi-damage-type attack** (e.g. a weapon with a bonus elemental damage die of a different type than the base weapon damage) — both dice pools double under §1.2's "all the attack's damage dice," but resistance/vulnerability/immunity must be evaluated **per damage type**, independently, against the target — this means `character_attacks`/monster `statBlockEntrySchema` rows that only support ONE `damageType` per action row (confirmed: `statBlockEntrySchema` has exactly one `damageType: z.string().optional()` field, singular) **cannot represent a genuinely dual-typed single attack today** — flagging as a real, pre-existing schema gap this feature doesn't need to solve (most attacks are single-typed) but shouldn't accidentally make worse; out of scope to fix here, named so it's not silently assumed solved.
5. **Advantage/disadvantage crit determination** (§1.5) — the discarded die's value must never be inspected for crit purposes, even when it's a 20; the endpoint in §2.3 must receive `isCritical` computed by the caller from the KEPT d20 only, and `rollDice`'s existing `keptTotal` computation (`services/diceRolls.ts` lines 107–112) already produces exactly this value today for attack rolls — the new endpoint's crit determination should read `keptTotal === 20` off that same existing computation path, not re-derive it separately.
6. **Save-based attacks never crit** (§1.6) — the new `character_attacks`/monster-action rows with `saveDc` set must never accept an `isCritical: true` on the damage-application call; the endpoint (§2.3) should reject or ignore `isCritical` when the triggering roll was a save, not a d20 attack roll, since there is no SRD mechanism for a "critical save."
7. **Half-damage-on-save vs. resistance, together** (§1.4's ordering + §1.7) — a save-based effect that also hits a resistant target: the SRD ordering text (§1.3/§1.4, "flat adjustments first, then Resistance, then Vulnerability") doesn't explicitly slot "half on save" into that sequence, but since half-on-save (§1.7) is itself round-down and produces a flat total before type-based effects apply (it's not itself a "flat adjustment" in the 2024 ordering sentence's narrow sense, but functionally produces the pre-resistance raw total the same way a crit-doubled roll does), the natural reading is: roll damage → halve if saved successfully (round down) → THEN apply resistance/vulnerability/immunity to that already-halved number. Flagged as inference from the general pattern (raw total computed first, type-based effects applied last), not a directly-quoted combined rule.
8. **Temporary resistance sources (Rage) expiring mid-combat** (§2.4) — `computeAppliedDamage`'s caller must re-read `active_effects` fresh on every damage application, never cache a resistance set from an earlier point in the same encounter; a Rage ending between two attacks in the same round must not leave a stale resistance applied to the second attack.
9. **Zero and negative damage** — 2024 `combat.md` line 369 (implicitly referenced, general Damage Rolls text): "With a penalty, it is possible to deal 0 damage, but never negative damage." `computeAppliedDamage`'s `rawTotal` and post-resistance `appliedDamage` must both clamp at 0, never go negative (a resisted 1-point hit rounds down to 0, which is correct and distinct from a rejected/invalid roll).
10. **Character resistance columns default to empty, not null** — `TEXT[] NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'` (§2.4) so every existing character row and every new one has a well-defined empty array rather than requiring a null-check at every call site, matching the monsters table's own existing column convention exactly.

### 4. What must be tested

Server-side `*.integration.test.ts` (matching `packages/server/src/services/encounters.actionEconomyAuthz.integration.test.ts`'s convention), targeting crafted-API-call bypass attempts specifically, not just the happy path:

- **Client cannot bypass resistance by pre-computing a final delta**: a crafted `PATCH /monster-instances/:id/hp` call with a client-supplied `delta` that ignores the monster's known fire resistance must still apply exactly the client's number (proving the OLD endpoint is deliberately dumb/trusting) — paired with a second assertion that the NEW `apply-damage` endpoint, given the same raw dice/type against the same resistant target, produces the correctly-halved result, demonstrating the new endpoint is the one that must be used for any damage-type-aware application, not a claim that the old endpoint got "fixed."
- **Critical hit doubles dice, not modifier**: `apply-damage` with `isCritical: true`, a `1d8+3` attack, damage type with no resistance → asserts the rolled-dice component reflects 2d8 (verifiable via a seeded/mocked RNG or by asserting the returned breakdown's `diceTotal` falls in the 2–16 range consistent with 2d8, never 1–8) while `modifier` in the breakdown is exactly 3, not 6.
- **Order of operations: crit doubling before resistance**: seed a resistant target, force (mock) a specific dice outcome under `isCritical: true`, assert `appliedDamage === floor((doubledDiceTotal + modifier) / 2)` — specifically a test that would FAIL under the wrong order (halve-then-double-dice) to prove the ordering, not just that resistance is applied somewhere.
- **Resistance alone**: non-critical hit, resistant target → `appliedDamage === floor(rawTotal / 2)`, asserting round-down on an odd raw total specifically (e.g. raw 7 → applied 3, not 4).
- **Vulnerability alone**: non-critical hit, vulnerable target → `appliedDamage === rawTotal * 2`.
- **Resistance then vulnerability on the same type**: target with both arrays containing the same type → `appliedDamage === floor(rawTotal / 2) * 2` per the confirmed ordering (§1.4/§2.2) — this is the sharpest regression risk in this whole feature per this doc's own §1.4 flag, deserving its own dedicated test with a specific odd `rawTotal` where naive "they cancel" logic (`appliedDamage === rawTotal`) would visibly diverge from the literal-order result.
- **Multiple sources of the same resistance don't stack to quarter damage**: target's `damage_resistances` array contains the same type twice (or resistance from both permanent columns and an active effect union to the same type) → `appliedDamage === floor(rawTotal / 2)`, not `floor(rawTotal / 4)`.
- **Immunity zeroes regardless of vulnerability also being present** (edge case 2): target immune AND (erroneously/homebrew) vulnerable to the same type → `appliedDamage === 0`.
- **Untyped damage ignores all three arrays**: `damageType: null` against a target resistant/vulnerable/immune to everything else → `appliedDamage === rawTotal` unchanged.
- **Advantage crit uses the kept die only**: mock the d20 roll to produce `[20, 15]` under advantage → `isCritical === true` (kept die is 20); mock `[15, 20]` under **disadvantage** → `isCritical === false` (kept die is 15, the 20 is discarded) — this exact case is named in the task brief as the one to get right, and deserves a test asserting the discarded-20 case explicitly, not just the straightforward advantage-crit case.
- **Save-based attacks reject or ignore `isCritical: true`**: a crafted `apply-damage` call against a `character_attacks`/monster-action row where the triggering context is a save (not an attack roll) with `isCritical: true` forced in the request body → server must not double the dice; proves the endpoint doesn't trust a client-asserted crit flag for a roll shape that can't produce one.
- **Zero-floor, never negative**: a 1-point raw hit against a resistant target → `appliedDamage === 0`, never `-0` or a negative number reaching `applyHpDeltaWithTempAbsorption`.
- **Temp-HP absorption still runs correctly downstream of resistance**: a resisted damage total that's fully absorbed by existing temp HP → `hp_current` unchanged, `hp_temp` reduced by exactly the post-resistance (not pre-resistance) amount — proving `computeAppliedDamage`'s output, not the raw roll, is what's fed into `applyHpDeltaWithTempAbsorption`.
- **Ownership/authz on the new endpoint matches the existing `PATCH .../hp` rules exactly**: a player attempting `apply-damage` against another player's character, or any player attempting it against a monster instance → `403`, mirroring the existing HP-endpoint authorization tests rather than introducing a parallel, potentially-looser check.
- **Row locking**: two rapid concurrent `apply-damage` calls against the same character (simulating a double-click or a monster's multiattack landing two hits in quick succession) must serialize correctly (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`) so temp-HP absorption for the second hit sees the first hit's already-updated state, not a stale read — same race class `docs/rules/movement.md` §2.4 already flags for `setParticipantPosition`.
- **Rage-style temporary resistance expires correctly**: apply damage of a Rage-resisted type while an active "Raging" effect is present → halved; remove the effect; apply identical damage again → full, unhalved — proving the union is read fresh per call, not cached (edge case 8).

## DM-configurable, never hardcoded

Named explicitly per this repo's persona convention:

- **Whether a save-based effect's damage is halved or negated entirely on a success** (§2.1's `half_on_save` column) — the SRD default is "half on success" for most effects, but not universal (some effects explicitly say "no damage on a success" in their own spell/action text) — this is per-attack-row DM/build-time content, not a single global toggle, hence a column on `character_attacks`/`statBlockEntrySchema` rather than a `campaigns` setting.
- **Resistance/vulnerability/immunity arrays themselves** (§2.4) — inherently DM-authored per-character/per-monster content (a Dwarf PC's poison resistance is chosen at character creation, a homebrew monster's resistances are chosen when the DM builds the stat block) — not a rules-engine toggle, but flagging that the UI must expose these as freely-editable arrays (matching the existing homebrew-monster form's treatment of the same three fields) rather than a fixed race/class-derived computed list, since 5e explicitly allows DM-granted exceptions beyond what a race/class table would compute automatically.
- **Both-resistance-and-vulnerability-to-the-same-type computation** (§1.4, §3 edge case 3) — since this specific combination isn't independently SRD-confirmed beyond the general ordering rule, a DM who wants the commonly-house-ruled "they cancel out entirely" behavior instead of this doc's literal-order computation should get that as an explicit, named alternative if requested later — not proposing a new `campaigns` column for this now (no signal any campaign in this app has hit this rare case yet), but flagging it as the kind of thing that would need one if it comes up, rather than silently picking one behavior and calling it settled forever.
