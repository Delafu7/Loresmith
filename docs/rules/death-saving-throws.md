# Death Saving Throws

## The 0-HP state machine: falling unconscious, death saves, stabilizing, damage-at-0-HP, instant death

Consulted for: `docs/roadmap/dnd-2024-gap-analysis.md` **P1-1** ("Death saving throw state machine — track success/failure counts on `characters`, apply the natural-20/natural-1/damage-at-0-HP rules, and stabilization"). ### 1. Official rule

#### 1.1 When death saves start

**2014** `combat.md` lines 441–443 ("Falling Unconscious"): "If damage reduces you to 0 hit points and fails to kill you, you fall unconscious... This unconsciousness ends if you regain any hit points." Line 447 ("Death Saving Throws"): "**Whenever you start your turn with 0 hit points**, you must make a special saving throw, called a death saving throw... Unlike other saving throws, this one isn't tied to any ability score."

**2024** `combat.md` lines 147–154 (condensed): "Otherwise the character falls **Unconscious** and starts making **Death Saving Throws**: roll 1d20 each turn at 0 HP (no ability modifier)."

**Verdict: identical trigger in both editions — a death save is rolled at the start of each of your own turns while you are at 0 HP**, not the instant you drop to 0. **Edge case this implies, not itself a directly-quoted sentence**: the turn *during which* you're reduced to 0 HP does not itself trigger a death save — "start your turn with 0 hit points" is a state check made only at the moment a turn begins, and dropping to 0 happens after that check already passed for the current turn (whether it's your own turn mid-action or someone else's turn). The first death save happens at the start of your **next** turn. Neither edition's grounding text states this "no save on the turn you drop" case as its own sentence — it's the direct logical reading of "whenever you start your turn," flagged as inferred, not a separate quote.

#### 1.2 The saving throw itself — DC, modifiers

**2014** `combat.md` line 447: "this one isn't tied to any ability score. You are in the hands of fate now, aided only by spells and features that improve your chances of succeeding on a saving throw." Line 449: "Roll a d20. If the roll is 10 or higher, you succeed. Otherwise, you fail."

**2024** `combat.md` line 153: "roll 1d20 each turn at 0 HP (**no ability modifier**); 10+ is a success."

**Verdict: flat DC 10, no ability modifier, no proficiency bonus (base rules define no proficiency for this specific roll).** It is still, mechanically, "a saving throw" for the purposes of any other rule that references saving throws generically — 2014's own text says so explicitly ("aided only by spells and features that improve your chances of succeeding on a saving throw"), meaning a feature/spell that grants advantage or a bonus "on saving throws" (e.g. *Bless*'s +1d4, a feature granting advantage on saves) does apply to a death save even though ability-modifier-based bonuses have nothing to add (mod is never consulted at all). **Not itself SRD-catalog data this doc can enumerate** (which specific class features/feats grant this) — flagged as an extensibility point for the schema (§2), not resolved here.

#### 1.3 Success/failure counting, nat 1, nat 20

**2014** `combat.md` line 449: "A success or failure has no effect by itself. **On your third success, you become stable** (see below). **On your third failure, you die.** The successes and failures don't need to be consecutive; keep track of both until you collect three of a kind. **The number of both is reset to zero when you regain any hit points or become stable.**" Line 451: "***Rolling 1 or 20.*** When you make a death saving throw and roll a 1 on the d20, **it counts as two failures**. If you roll a 20 on the d20, **you regain 1 hit point**."

**2024** `combat.md` lines 153–155 (condensed): "Three successes = Stable; three failures = dead. A natural 20 restores 1 HP; a natural 1 counts as two failures."

**Verdict: identical in both editions.** 3 successes (non-consecutive, tracked independently from failures) → Stable. 3 failures (non-consecutive) → dead. Natural 1 on the d20 = 2 failures in one roll (can end the sequence in a single roll if already at 2 failures). Natural 20 = regain 1 HP outright (this is **not** "an automatic success" being counted — it bypasses the counters entirely by ending the 0-HP state: regaining any HP resets both counters per the sentence above and ends unconsciousness per §1.1's "This unconsciousness ends if you regain any hit points"). A natural 20 is functionally a full exit from the death-save state machine in one roll, not merely "3 successes' worth" of progress.

#### 1.4 What resets the counters

**2014** `combat.md` line 449 (already quoted in §1.3): "The number of both is reset to zero when you regain **any** hit points or become stable." Confirmed **two distinct reset triggers**: (a) regaining any HP (from a nat-20 death save, healing magic, a potion, etc. — a single point is sufficient, "any"), or (b) becoming Stable (successful 3rd death-save success, or the DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) Help-action stabilization of §1.6 — both routes to "stable" reset the counters, since a stable creature "doesn't make death saving throws" at all per §1.6).

No separate reset condition exists in either edition's grounding text beyond these two — specifically, **taking damage while at 0 HP does NOT reset the counters to zero**; it adds a failure (or two, per §1.5) on top of whatever the counts already were.

#### 1.5 Damage taken while at 0 HP — failure(s), and instant death via "massive damage"

**2014** `combat.md` line 453: "***Damage at 0 Hit Points.*** If you take any damage while you have 0 hit points, you suffer a death saving throw failure. If the damage is from a critical hit, you suffer two failures instead. If the damage equals or exceeds your hit point maximum, you suffer instant death."

**2024** `combat.md` lines 155–157 (condensed): "Taking any damage at 0 HP is an automatic failure (two if it's a Critical Hit); if that damage equals/exceeds your HP maximum, you die."

**Verdict: identical in both editions.** Three distinct outcomes from one incoming hit while at 0 HP, evaluated together, not as alternatives you pick between:
1. Any damage ⇒ 1 death-save failure, always (never "no effect" — there is no damage-at-0-HP amount too small to count).
2. If that damage came from a **critical hit** ⇒ 2 failures instead of 1 (same doubling this app's `computeAppliedDamage`/attack pipeline already tracks via `isCritical`, per `docs/rules/attacks-and-damage.md` §1.2).
3. **Independently of (1)/(2)**, if the damage amount **equals or exceeds the creature's hit point maximum**, the creature dies outright (instant death, bypassing the failure-counter entirely — this is not "a 3rd failure," it's a separate kill condition that can trigger on the very first hit at 0 HP).

**Exact threshold formula, unified across both trigger contexts** (initial drop to 0, and subsequent hits while already at 0): the SRD states this "massive damage" threshold twice, in slightly different phrasing, for what is the same underlying formula:
- **Initial drop to 0** — `combat.md` lines 435–439 (2014, "### Instant Death"): "Massive damage can kill you instantly. When damage reduces you to 0 hit points and there is damage remaining, **you die if the remaining damage equals or exceeds your hit point maximum**." Worked example given (line 439): a 12-max-HP cleric at 6 current HP takes 18 damage → reduced to 0, 12 damage "remaining" (18 − 6) → remaining (12) equals max (12) → dies.
- **Subsequent hit already at 0** — line 453 (quoted above): "If the damage equals or exceeds your hit point maximum, you suffer instant death" — here the comparison is against the **full** incoming damage amount, because there is no positive `hp_current` buffer left to subtract first.

These are **the same formula applied consistently**, not two different rules: `overkill = incomingDamage − hp_current_immediately_before_this_hit` (floored at the full incoming damage when `hp_current` is already 0, since there is nothing to subtract). **If `overkill >= hp_max`, the creature dies instantly**, regardless of which of the two contexts it happened in. This unification is this doc's own derivation from chaining the two quoted passages — flagged as inferred-by-consistency, not a single sentence stating the unified formula, though the 2014 text's own wording ("remaining damage") and the 0-HP text's wording ("the damage" — which, at 0 HP, *is* the remaining damage in full) support the same read.

#### 1.6 Stabilizing a creature

**2014** `combat.md` lines 455–461: "The best way to save a creature with 0 hit points is to heal it. If healing is unavailable, the creature can at least be stabilized so that it isn't killed by a failed death saving throw. **You can use your action to administer first aid to an unconscious creature and attempt to stabilize it, which requires a successful DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check.** A **stable** creature doesn't make death saving throws, even though it has 0 hit points, but it does remain unconscious. **The creature stops being stable, and must start making death saving throws again, if it takes any damage.** A stable creature that isn't healed **regains 1 hit point after 1d4 hours**."

**2024** `combat.md` lines 158–160 (condensed): "Stabilizing: **the Help action** with a successful DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check stabilizes a creature at 0 HP (stops saves, still Unconscious). A Stable, unhealed creature regains 1 HP after 1d4 hours."

**Verdict: mechanically identical** (uses an action, DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine), remains unconscious at 0 HP, regains 1 HP after 1d4 hours if never healed in the meantime). Minor wording-only difference: 2024 explicitly names this "the Help action" (tying it to that named action's taxonomy); 2014's grounding text here just says "use your action," without invoking the "Help" action name in this passage. Not a mechanical difference — both require an action and the same DC 10 Wis (Medicine) check.

**Important distinction from §1.2**: the DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check to *stabilize* is a normal ability check (proficiency bonus and Wisdom modifier apply normally, per this app's existing ability-check rules) — it is **not** the death save itself, which per §1.2 uses no modifiers at all. Don't conflate "no modifiers" (true only of the death save d20 roll) with the stabilization check (a normal, modified ability check).

"Stable" also stops being stable — and the creature must resume making death saves — the instant it takes **any** damage (2014 line 461, quoted above), which is a third, narrower reset trigger distinct from §1.4's two counter-reset triggers: taking damage while Stable doesn't just fail to reset the counters, it **removes Stable status outright** and puts the creature back into the active death-save loop (with counters presumably starting fresh at 0/0, since Stable itself was already a reset-to-zero state per §1.4 — the SRD text doesn't explicitly restate "and the counters are 0" here, but that's the necessary consequence of Stable having already zeroed them when it was reached).

#### 1.7 Attacking a creature at 0 HP — advantage, and automatic critical hits

**2014** `conditions.md` lines 95–105 ("## Unconscious"): "An unconscious creature is incapacitated..., can't move or speak, and is unaware of its surroundings. The creature drops whatever it's holding and falls prone. The creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws. **Attack rolls against the creature have advantage.**" ... "**Any attack that hits the creature is a critical hit if the attacker is within 1.5 meters (5 ft) of the creature.**"

**2024** `conditions.md` lines 104–113 ("## Unconscious"): "**Inert.** You have the Incapacitated and Prone conditions, and you drop whatever you're holding... **Speed 0.** Your Speed is 0 and can't increase. **Attacks Affected.** Attack rolls against you have Advantage. **Saving Throws Affected.** You automatically fail Strength and Dexterity saving throws. **Automatic Critical Hits.** Any attack roll that hits you is a Critical Hit if the attacker is within 1.5 meters (5 ft) of you. **Unaware.** You're unaware of your surroundings."

**Verdict: identical mechanical outcome in both editions** — every attack roll against an unconscious (0-HP, non-Stable-or-Stable, doesn't matter — Unconscious is Unconscious either way) creature has **advantage**, unconditionally, regardless of range; and **any hit** becomes an **automatic critical hit** specifically when the attacker is within 5 ft/1.5 m (adjacent) — this covers melee attacks (always adjacent by definition) and any ranged/spell attack made from an adjacent square. This auto-crit interacts directly with §1.5: an adjacent attacker who hits a 0-HP creature triggers **both** the auto-crit damage math (`docs/rules/attacks-and-damage.md` §1.2, dice doubled) **and** the "2 failures instead of 1" branch of the damage-at-0-HP death-save rule, together, from the same single hit.

2024's text additionally spells out (as its own named bullet, "Speed 0") that Unconscious sets Speed to 0 — 2014's grounding text doesn't restate this as its own bullet under Unconscious (2014 folds "can't move" into the Unconscious condition's own opening sentence instead: "can't move or speak"). **Wording-structure difference only, not a mechanical difference** — both editions leave an unconscious creature unable to move, whichever condition-text bullet states it.

#### 1.8 Knocking a creature out — 2014 vs. 2024 **mechanical difference**

**2014** `combat.md` lines 469–471 ("### Knocking a Creature Out"): "When an attacker reduces a creature to 0 hit points **with a melee attack**, the attacker can knock the creature out. The attacker can make this choice the instant the damage is dealt. **The creature falls unconscious and is stable.**"

**2024** `combat.md` lines 143–145 (condensed, under "Damage and Healing"): "**Knocking a creature out:** a melee attack that would drop a creature to 0 HP can instead **leave it at 1 HP** with the Unconscious condition, if the attacker chooses."

**This is a real, confirmed mechanical difference, not just wording — flag prominently:**
- **2014**: the knocked-out creature is left at **0 HP**, Unconscious, **and Stable** — it never enters the death-save loop at all (Stable creatures don't roll death saves per §1.6), and it still needs the 1d4-hour natural-recovery or actual healing to wake up.
- **2024**: the knocked-out creature is left at **1 HP** (not 0), with the Unconscious condition applied directly — it is **not** at 0 HP, so it is not Stable (Stable is only meaningful at 0 HP), does not need the 1d4-hour recovery, and would need to be reduced to 0 HP again by a subsequent hit before death saves become relevant at all. It's simply an unconscious creature with a positive HP total.

**Only available for melee attacks** in the grounding text of both editions ("with a melee attack" / "a melee attack"), and only for damage that would otherwise reduce the target from above 0 to 0 (an attacker choosing this option on a creature already at 0 HP is not a described case in either edition's grounding text — not addressed either way).

#### 1.9 Monsters and death — both editions, and this is DM-configurable

**2014** `combat.md` lines 463–467 ("### Monsters and Death"): "Most GMs have a monster die the instant it drops to 0 hit points, rather than having it fall unconscious and make death saving throws. Mighty villains and special nonplayer characters are common exceptions; the GM might have them fall unconscious and follow the same rules as player characters."

**2024** `combat.md` line 148 (condensed): "A **monster** dies instantly at 0 HP (GM may rule otherwise for a specific monster)."

**Verdict: identical in both editions, and explicitly framed by the SRD itself as a GM/DM per-creature judgment call, not a hard rule** — "most GMs," "GM may rule otherwise." **This is DM-configurable, named explicitly per this project's own convention** (see the "DM-configurable" section below) — it is not settled by the SRD as "monsters always die instantly," only as the *common default*, with named PC-style exceptions.

#### 1.10 Temporary HP granted while at 0 HP — does not wake or stabilize

**2014** `combat.md` line 483: "If you have 0 hit points, receiving temporary hit points doesn't restore you to consciousness or stabilize you. They can still absorb damage directed at you while you're in that state, but only true healing can save you." No equivalent sentence found in this skill's 2024 `combat.md` grounding text (the 2024 file in this skill is a condensed rewrite and doesn't restate the full "Temporary Hit Points" prose section) — **not claiming 2024 lacks this rule, only that this specific grounding file doesn't restate it**; flagged rather than assumed identical without a citation.

**Confirmed textual gap, flagged rather than invented**: neither edition's grounding text states whether damage that is **fully absorbed by temporary HP** while the creature is at 0 HP still counts as "tak[ing] any damage" for the purposes of §1.5's automatic death-save failure. Temp HP is HP-pool math (`docs/rules/attacks-and-damage.md`'s domain), and §1.5's failure trigger is phrased as "take any damage," not "lose any hit points" — these could plausibly be read as the same event or as two different ones. **This doc does not resolve this ambiguity by invented specificity** — it's named here as an open question the calling session should decide and document as an explicit implementation choice (most likely candidate reading, unconfirmed: any damage instance that connects at all — even if fully absorbed by temp HP — still counts as "taking damage" for the death-save-failure trigger, since the creature was still hit; but this is this doc's own best-guess flag, not an SRD-sourced answer).

### 2. Data model translation

#### 2.1 New columns — `characters` table

`characters` (`1784269737666_create-characters.ts`) already has `hp_current`/`hp_max`/`hp_temp`/`is_alive`, but `is_alive` is a bare boolean that can't represent the three real states a 0-HP creature can be in (actively dying / stable / dead) — and it's confirmed **never written after insert today** (`services/characters.ts` sets it only in the initial `INSERT` column list; no `UPDATE ... SET is_alive` exists anywhere in that file). Add:

```sql
ALTER TABLE characters ADD COLUMN death_save_successes SMALLINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 CHECK (death_save_successes BETWEEN 0 AND 3);
ALTER TABLE characters ADD COLUMN death_save_failures  SMALLINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 CHECK (death_save_failures  BETWEEN 0 AND 3);
ALTER TABLE characters ADD COLUMN is_stable            BOOLEAN  NOT NULL DEFAULT false;
-- is_alive (existing column) becomes the "dead" terminal state — set false on
-- the 3rd failure or on a confirmed massive-damage instant-death check (§1.5).
-- It is NOT reused for "unconscious" or "stable" — those are is_stable=false/
-- true while is_alive=true and hp_current=0, a DISTINCT state from is_alive=false.
```

Real `SMALLINT` columns (not JSONB) per this app's own precedent (`PLAN.md` §3.3, followed exactly by `active_effects.stack_count`, `characters.exhaustion_level`): these values are read, compared, and reset by server logic on every single damage/heal/turn-advance call — exactly the "filtered/sorted/joined and needs a real column" case, not "genuinely variable, unqueried structure."

**Why not model "Stable" as an `active_effects` row instead of a `characters` column**: confirmed via `python3 .opencode/skills/dnd5e-srd/scripts/query.py 2014/2024 conditions --list` that **"Stable" is not one of the 15 catalog conditions** in either edition (Blinded, Charmed, Deafened, Exhaustion, Frightened, Grappled, Incapacitated, Invisible, Paralyzed, Petrified, Poisoned, Prone, Restrained, Stunned, Unconscious — no "Stable"). It is a distinct game state described only in the death-saves rule text, not a condition a spell/effect grants or removes — so it does not belong in `active_effects` (which references `effect_definitions`, itself sourced from the conditions/spells catalog). **Unconscious**, by contrast, **is** a real catalog condition (§1.7) and should be applied as a normal `active_effects` row (source_type `'manual'` or a new `'zero_hp'`-flavored source, DM/system-applied) exactly like any other condition — `is_stable` only gates *whether death saves are still rolled while Unconscious+0HP*, it is not itself a condition row.

#### 2.2 New columns — `monster_instances` table, and the DM-configurable default

`monster_instances.status` (`1784269739666_create-monster-instances.ts`) already has `'alive'|'dead'|'fled'|'captured'` — sufficient for the RAW default of §1.9 (monster dies instantly at 0 HP → set `status = 'dead'`), but insufficient for the named "mighty villain" exception, which needs the exact same three-state death-save machinery characters get. Recommended:

```sql
ALTER TABLE monster_instances ADD COLUMN uses_death_saves      BOOLEAN  NOT NULL DEFAULT false;
ALTER TABLE monster_instances ADD COLUMN death_save_successes  SMALLINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 CHECK (death_save_successes BETWEEN 0 AND 3);
ALTER TABLE monster_instances ADD COLUMN death_save_failures   SMALLINT NOT NULL DEFAULT 0 CHECK (death_save_failures  BETWEEN 0 AND 3);
ALTER TABLE monster_instances ADD COLUMN is_stable             BOOLEAN  NOT NULL DEFAULT false;
-- status transitions to 'dead' exactly as it does today when uses_death_saves
-- is false (§1.9 default); the death-save loop only ever runs at all when
-- uses_death_saves is true for that specific instance.
```

`uses_death_saves` defaults `false`, reproducing today's implicit behavior (`status` already defaults toward instant-death semantics with no unconscious state modeled at all) — matching this app's own stated precedent for a new toggle (`1784269760666_add-campaign-ability-reroll-setting.ts`'s own comment: "Defaults to true, reproducing today's implicit behavior"; here the implicit behavior being reproduced is instant death, so the new flag defaults the other way, `false`).

**This flag is per-`monster_instance` row, not a `campaigns`-wide setting** — the SRD's own wording ("Mighty villains and special nonplayer characters are **common exceptions**") frames this as a per-creature DM decision (one specific BBEG in one specific encounter), not a table-wide policy. **DM-configurable, named explicitly**: exposing `uses_death_saves` as a DM-only editable field on a monster instance's sheet (same authorization tier as any other DM-only monster-instance field) is the concrete UI hook this rule implies; there is currently no campaign-wide equivalent proposed here since no SRD text suggests a table would want *every* monster to use death saves by default, but if a future request needs that, it would be a new `campaigns.*` boolean column, per this app's own established per-toggle-column precedent — not retrofitted into this same column.

#### 2.3 Where the 0-HP transition and damage-at-0-HP logic must live — server-side, inside `applyDamage`

**Critical validation must live server-side inside the existing damage-application transaction, not the client.** `services/characters.ts`'s `applyDamage` (lines 640–780) already does the one thing that matters most for this rule: it locks the row `FOR UPDATE`, computes `hp_current`/`hp_temp` via `applyHpDeltaWithTempAbsorption` (`services/hp.ts`), and writes the result — **all of §1.1–§1.10's logic must be added inside that same locked transaction**, immediately after computing the new `hpCurrent`/`hpTemp` and before `COMMIT`, using the **pre-damage** `hp_current`/`hp_max` (already available as `row.hp_current`/`row.hp_max` in that function) to compute the overkill/massive-damage formula from §1.5 — a client-side-only "you died" message with no matching server enforcement would let a crafted API call skip the mark-dead write entirely, which is exactly this project's own named anti-pattern.

Concretely, the new logic block (both for `characters.applyDamage` and its `monster_instances` sibling, `applyMonsterInstanceDamage` in `services/monsters.ts`) needs, in order, per hit:
1. **Compute overkill** = `appliedDamage - Math.max(0, row.hp_current)` (§1.5's unified formula) using the row's pre-hit `hp_current` (0 if already at 0 HP — the formula degrades correctly to "full applied damage" in that case without a separate branch).
2. **If `overkill >= hp_max`** → instant death: set `is_alive = false` (characters) / `status = 'dead'` (monster instances not using death saves, or even those that are — massive damage kills regardless of the death-save state), skip all further death-save bookkeping, `hp_current` still clamps to 0 via the existing `applyHpDeltaWithTempAbsorption` math.
3. **Else if this hit reduced `hp_current` to exactly 0 from a positive number** (the "falling unconscious" transition, §1.1) → apply the `Unconscious` condition (a real `active_effects` row, §2.1) and leave `death_save_successes`/`death_save_failures`/`is_stable` at their already-zeroed defaults — this is a fresh 0-HP entry, not a continuation.
4. **Else if `row.hp_current` was already 0 before this hit** (§1.5, "damage at 0 HP") and the character/monster-instance is `is_alive = true` and `is_stable = false` → increment `death_save_failures` by 1, or by 2 if `isCritical` (the same `isCritical` value `applyDamage` already derives server-side per `docs/rules/attacks-and-damage.md` §2.2 — reuse it, don't re-derive); if `is_stable = true`, instead **clear** `is_stable` back to `false` (§1.6's "stops being stable... if it takes any damage") and leave the counters at their already-reset 0/0 (no failure is added on the hit that *breaks* stability itself — the SRD text only says stability ends, not that the breaking hit also counts as the first failure of the new sequence; **flagged as this doc's own reading of §1.6's silence on this specific interaction, not a directly quoted combined sentence**).
5. **If `death_save_failures` reaches 3** at any point in steps 2/4 → `is_alive = false` (die).

**Rolling the death save itself** (as opposed to damage arriving at 0 HP) is a **separate action from `applyDamage`** — it's a player/DM-initiated d20 roll (already labeled `'death_save'` in `dice_rolls.roll_type`, currently unused for anything beyond the label) that must itself write to `death_save_successes`/`death_save_failures`/`is_stable`. Recommended new endpoint, `POST /characters/:id/death-save`, body `{}` (no client-supplied roll — rolled server-side, same "RNG lives here and only here" invariant `docs/rules/attacks-and-damage.md` §2.3 already establishes for damage dice), which:
1. Authorizes exactly as `applyHpDelta` does (character's own controller, or DM).
2. **Rejects (409/422) if `hp_current !== 0` or `is_alive === false` or `is_stable === true`** — a death save is illegal to roll under any of those conditions (§1.1's trigger condition, and §1.6's "a stable creature doesn't make death saving throws" are both hard preconditions, not soft UI hints).
3. Rolls 1d20 server-side, inserts the roll into `dice_rolls` with `roll_type = 'death_save'` (finally giving that existing label a real writer).
4. Applies §1.3's counting rules (nat 1 = +2 failures, nat 20 = regain 1 HP and reset both counters to 0 and clear the 0-HP state entirely — this is a `hp_current` write too, not just a counter reset — 10+ = +1 success, else +1 failure), then checks the 3-success (→ `is_stable = true`, counters reset to 0 per §1.4) and 3-failure (→ `is_alive = false`) thresholds.
5. Must be called with the same `FOR UPDATE` row-lock discipline as `applyHpDelta`/`applyDamage` — two near-simultaneous death-save rolls for the same character (e.g. a double-click) must serialize, not race to both read "2 failures" and both independently write "3rd failure," which would be harmless here (idempotent-ish, still ends at 3) but the same race class is genuinely dangerous for the 3-success→stable transition landing concurrently with an incoming damage-at-0HP failure from `applyDamage` — those two code paths **must** take out the same row lock or a stabilize-then-immediately-un-stabilize ordering could be lost or duplicated.

**Stabilizing via the Help action / DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check (§1.6)** is presently **entirely unimplemented** — no "Medicine" or "first aid"/"stabilize" logic exists anywhere in `services/*.ts` (confirmed by search). This needs its own endpoint (e.g. `POST /characters/:targetId/stabilize` with a `helperCharacterId`/roll-context, authorized as an action-economy-consuming action by the helper, not the target) that rolls a Wisdom (Medicine) check (normal ability-check modifiers, per §1.6's own explicit distinction from the modifier-less death save) against DC 10, and on success sets `is_stable = true` + resets both counters, mirroring step 4 above's "3 successes" branch but reached via a different route.

**1d4-hour natural stabilized recovery (§1.6)**: this is a real-time/game-time clock rule this app currently has no obvious hook for (no existing "hours pass" trigger was found in this grounding pass — out of scope to solve here, but flagged as a genuine gap: a Stable character currently has no path back to 1 HP without an explicit heal unless a DM-facing "advance time" mechanism exists elsewhere in the app to hang a 1d4-hour roll off of).

#### 2.4 The "start of turn" hook — `advanceTurn`

Per §1.1, a death save is due "whenever you start your turn with 0 hit points" — the only place in this codebase that already models "whose turn is starting" is `services/encounters.ts`'s `advanceTurn` (lines 2119–2200), which resolves `nextParticipantId`/`character_id` for the participant whose turn is about to begin and resets their action economy in the same transaction. **The death-save-is-due prompt (not the roll itself — the roll stays a distinct player/DM-initiated action per §2.3) should be computed here**: after resolving `nextParticipantId`, check whether that participant's linked `character_id` (or `monster_instance_id`, if `uses_death_saves`) has `hp_current === 0 AND is_alive === true AND is_stable === false`, and if so include a `deathSaveDue: true` flag in `AdvanceTurnResult` for the route to broadcast — the actual d20 roll remains a separate call to the §2.3 endpoint, not something `advanceTurn` performs automatically (matching this app's existing pattern of *prompting* for player-driven rolls — e.g. the existing `CONCENTRATION_CHECK_PROMPTED` broadcast in `applyDamage` — rather than auto-rolling on the player's behalf).

#### 2.5 `computeAppliedDamage` does not change; the caller does

`services/damage.ts`'s `computeAppliedDamage` (resistance/vulnerability/immunity math) needs **no changes** — it already returns `appliedDamage`, which is exactly the number §2.3's overkill formula needs. All new logic belongs in the **caller** (`applyDamage` in `services/characters.ts`, and its monster-instance sibling), immediately after `computeAppliedDamage` returns and before the `UPDATE characters SET hp_current = ...` write, using the same transaction and the same `row.hp_current`/`row.hp_max` already fetched by the existing `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`.

### 3. Edge cases

1. **No death save on the turn you drop to 0** (§1.1) — the character who is reduced to 0 HP mid-turn (their own or an enemy's) does not roll a death save until the start of their *next* turn; `advanceTurn`'s prompt (§2.4) must not fire for the same turn-index the 0-HP transition happened on.
2. **Nat 20 fully exits the state, doesn't just add "3 successes"** (§1.3) — a nat-20 death save must write `hp_current = 1` (regain 1 HP — starting from 0, "regain 1 hit point" always lands on exactly 1, never higher, since `hp_current` was 0 and this isn't capped by `hp_max` at that magnitude) AND reset both counters to 0 AND clear `is_stable`/remove the Unconscious `active_effects` row, in the same write — not merely increment `death_save_successes` and separately check "is it ≥3."
3. **Nat 1 alone can end the sequence** if the character already has 2 failures — a single roll of nat-1 must be able to go straight from 2→dead (add 2, hit 3, check threshold), not clamp at "1 failure per roll."
4. **Damage-at-0-HP failures happen on ANY hit, including ones from an ally** (e.g. an AoE spell that also catches the downed character) — §1.5's text says "any damage," with no attacker-relationship carve-out; friendly-fire damage at 0 HP still triggers a failure (and still needs the massive-damage overkill check) exactly like enemy damage, if this app's damage pipeline permits PC-vs-PC/ally damage at all (per `docs/rules/attacks-and-damage.md`'s own noted "not-yet-decided scope question" on friendly fire — this doc doesn't resolve that scope question, only notes that IF such damage is applied, it must run through the same 0-HP logic, not a bypass path).
5. **Massive damage on the very first hit that drops a character can kill before any death save is ever rolled** — a character at full HP one-shot for damage ≥ (current HP + max HP) never becomes Unconscious at all; `is_alive` goes straight to `false`, no `active_effects` Unconscious row is ever created. Don't create-then-immediately-remove an Unconscious condition row for this case.
6. **Temp HP absorption happens before the massive-damage/failure check, using post-absorption damage** — `applyHpDeltaWithTempAbsorption` already subtracts temp HP first; the overkill formula (§1.5/§2.3) must use the damage that actually reached `hp_current` (i.e., `appliedDamage` minus whatever temp HP absorbed), not the pre-temp-HP raw total — a hit fully absorbed by temp HP while at 0 HP raises the open question flagged in §1.10 (does it even count as a death-save failure at all); a hit **partially** absorbed by temp HP, with a remainder that reaches `hp_current`, should almost certainly use only that remainder for both the failure-trigger and the massive-damage threshold, since a fully-temp-HP-absorbed instance clearly did not exceed `hp_max` (max HP is always ≥ 0, and 0 can never be ≥ a positive `hp_max`) — this sub-case at least resolves itself arithmetically even where §1.10's broader ambiguity doesn't.
7. **Knocking a creature out bypasses this entire state machine on that hit** (§1.8) — a melee attacker choosing nonlethal damage must short-circuit steps 2–5 of §2.3 entirely: **2014** sets `hp_current = 0, is_stable = true` directly (no death-save entry at all); **2024** sets `hp_current = 1` (never touches `is_stable`/death-save columns, since the creature isn't at 0 HP). This is exactly the kind of `campaigns.srd_edition`-branched behavior the task brief calls out by name (`services/rests.ts`/`services/concentration.ts` precedent) — the nonlethal-damage code path must read `srd_edition` and take one of two genuinely different write paths, not one path with a cosmetic difference.
8. **Knock-out choice is only valid for melee attacks that would otherwise reduce the target to exactly 0** — both editions' grounding text ties this to "reduces a creature to 0 hit points with a melee attack" / "a melee attack that would drop a creature to 0 HP." A ranged or spell attack, or a melee attack against a creature already below its max-but-still-above-0 HP that this hit would drop *past* 0 with leftover massive-damage-eligible overkill, is not addressed by either edition's text as still eligible for the choice versus being forced into the massive-damage math instead — flagged as unresolved by the grounding text rather than guessed; the safest reading (not independently confirmed) is that the attacker's choice happens "the instant the damage is dealt" (2014, explicit), i.e., before the massive-damage overkill check would otherwise apply, functionally letting a melee attacker choose knockout specifically to avoid an outright kill on a blow that would have exceeded the massive-damage threshold — but this is this doc's inference from ordering language, not a directly confirmed ruling.
9. **A Stable creature that regains temp HP is still Stable, still at 0 HP, still unconscious** (§1.10) — temp HP grants while Stable must not clear `is_stable` or wake the creature; only "any hit point" (real HP) regained does that (§1.1/§1.4).
10. **Breaking Stable via damage does not itself add a failure** (§2.3 step 4's flagged reading) — the hit that ends Stable status resets the creature into a fresh 0/0-counter death-save loop; whether that *specific* hit also independently counts toward the new sequence's first failure is this doc's flagged unresolved reading (leaning "no, the hit only breaks stability" per the SRD's silence on double-counting it) — implement with a code comment pointing back to this section, not presented as directly SRD-confirmed either way.
11. **A DM manually adjusting HP via the plain `applyHpDelta` (heal/manual correction) path, not `applyDamage`**, must still trigger the "regain any hit points resets counters" rule (§1.4) — this path currently has no death-save awareness at all (§2.3's new logic is described only for `applyDamage`); a positive `delta` through `applyHpDelta` reaching a character at 0 HP with active death-save counters must also reset `death_save_successes`/`death_save_failures` to 0 and clear `is_stable`/remove Unconscious, exactly as a nat-20 roll would — this is not itself a new SRD citation beyond §1.1/§1.4's "regain any hit points," but is called out because it's a second code path (not just `applyDamage`) that must implement the same reset, easy to miss if only the damage path is patched.
12. **Monster instances not opted into `uses_death_saves`** never enter any of this machinery — a monster reduced to 0 HP via `applyMonsterInstanceDamage` should go straight to `status = 'dead'` exactly as it presumably does today (RAW default, §1.9), with the entire §2.1–§2.4 apparatus skipped, not run-and-immediately-overridden.
13. **Reviving the truly dead requires "magic such as *revivify*"** (2014 `combat.md` line 429: "A creature that has died can't regain hit points until magic such as the *revivify* spell has restored it to life" — not independently re-confirmed in this skill's condensed 2024 grounding text, flagged per §1.10's citation-discipline convention) — once `is_alive = false`, no ordinary heal/HP-delta call should be able to bring `hp_current` back above 0; the plain `applyHpDelta` endpoint must reject (or no-op) a positive delta against a character with `is_alive = false`, distinct from — and a stricter check than — the 0-HP-but-alive death-save-reset case in edge case 11.

### 4. What must be tested

Server-side `*.integration.test.ts` (matching this repo's convention, e.g. `packages/server/src/services/characters.applyDamage.integration.test.ts`), targeting crafted-API-call bypass attempts specifically:

- **Dropping to 0 HP applies Unconscious and initializes zeroed counters**: a character at positive HP takes damage exactly equal to remaining HP (no overkill) → `hp_current === 0`, `is_alive === true`, `is_stable === false`, `death_save_successes === 0`, `death_save_failures === 0`, and an `active_effects` row for the Unconscious condition exists.
- **Massive damage on the initial drop kills instantly, no Unconscious row created**: damage where `overkill (damage − hp_current_before) >= hp_max` → `is_alive === false`, `hp_current === 0`, and **no** Unconscious `active_effects` row was ever inserted (proving the code path branches before creating one, not creates-then-removes).
- **Massive damage exactly at the threshold (`overkill === hp_max`) still kills** — "equals or exceeds," not "exceeds" — a boundary test at exactly `hp_max` overkill, distinct from one point under it (which must NOT kill, only reduce to 0 + Unconscious).
- **Damage at 0 HP adds exactly 1 failure on a non-critical hit**: character already at `hp_current === 0`, `is_alive === true`, `is_stable === false`, takes a small non-critical hit → `death_save_failures` increments by exactly 1, `hp_current` stays 0, `is_alive` stays true (below 3).
- **Critical hit at 0 HP adds 2 failures in one hit, and 2 failures can reach 3 and kill from a single crit if already at 1 failure**: seed `death_save_failures = 1`, apply a critical hit at 0 HP → `death_save_failures === 3` and `is_alive === false` in the same call — a test that would fail under a "cap increment at 1 per hit" bug.
- **Massive damage while already at 0 HP overrides the failure-counter path entirely**: character at 0 HP, `death_save_failures = 0`, takes a hit whose full damage `>= hp_max` → `is_alive === false` directly, not merely `death_save_failures` incremented — proving the two checks (massive damage vs. ordinary failure) are independent, not "massive damage just counts as an extra failure."
- **A crafted client-supplied "final delta" cannot bypass the massive-damage/failure logic**: since §2.1 doc's own precedent (`docs/rules/attacks-and-damage.md`) already establishes damage must go through the server-rolling `apply-damage`-style endpoint, add a companion assertion here that calling the **plain** `applyHpDelta`/`PATCH .../hp` endpoint with a large negative delta against a character at positive HP does **not** run any death-save logic at all (that endpoint is explicitly the "heal/manual correction" path, §2.3) — and a separate test proving the *damage-application* endpoint specifically cannot be called with a client-asserted `appliedDamage` number that skips the overkill computation (the computation must always be server-derived from the locked row's pre-hit `hp_current`, never trusted from the request body).
- **Death-save roll rejects when illegal** (§2.3 step 2): a `POST .../death-save` call when `hp_current !== 0`, or when `is_alive === false`, or when `is_stable === true` → each returns an error status and writes nothing, three separate test cases.
- **Nat 20 on a death save regains exactly 1 HP and fully clears state**: mock the server-side d20 roll to 20 → `hp_current === 1`, `death_save_successes === 0`, `death_save_failures === 0`, `is_stable === false`, Unconscious `active_effects` row removed — all in one assertion set, proving it's a full-state-clear, not merely "+1 success recorded."
- **Nat 1 on a death save at 2 existing failures kills in one roll**: seed `death_save_failures = 2`, mock the roll to 1 → `death_save_failures === 3` (not clamped at 3 by only adding 1) and `is_alive === false`.
- **3rd success stabilizes and resets both counters, including any accumulated failures**: seed `death_save_successes = 2`, `death_save_failures = 2`, mock the roll to 10+ (a plain success, not nat-20) → `death_save_successes === 0`, `death_save_failures === 0`, `is_stable === true` — proving failures are wiped on stabilization too, not just successes zeroed.
- **Stabilize-via-Medicine-check endpoint** (§2.3): a successful DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check against a 0-HP, non-Stable target → `is_stable === true`, counters reset to 0; a failed check → no state change (still eligible to try again, matching the SRD's lack of any "only once" restriction).
- **Breaking Stable on any damage, even 1 point, even from a non-critical hit**: a Stable character takes 1 damage → `is_stable === false`, `death_save_failures === 0` (per edge case 10's flagged reading — the breaking hit itself does not also count as the first failure), `hp_current` still 0.
- **Temp HP grant at 0 HP does not wake or stabilize** (§1.10/edge case 9): a character at 0 HP, `is_stable === false`, receives a temp-HP grant via `applyHpDelta`'s `tempDelta` path → `hp_current` stays 0, `is_stable` stays false, no death-save counters change, `hp_temp` increases per the existing `applyHpDeltaWithTempAbsorption` "keep the higher" rule.
- **`applyHpDelta` (heal path) at 0 HP with positive delta clears death-save state** (edge case 11): character at 0 HP with `death_save_successes = 1, death_save_failures = 1` receives a heal of any positive amount through the plain HP-delta endpoint → `hp_current > 0`, both counters reset to 0, `is_stable` cleared, Unconscious removed — proving the **non-damage** HP-change path also implements the reset, not only the death-save-roll and damage paths.
- **Dead characters cannot be healed back up through the ordinary heal endpoint** (edge case 13): `is_alive === false` character, positive delta via `applyHpDelta` → rejected (or explicit no-op), `hp_current` unchanged, distinct from the previous test's 0-HP-but-alive case which DOES accept the heal.
- **Knocking out — edition-branched, both editions tested separately** (edge case 7): a melee attack flagged nonlethal that would otherwise reduce the target to 0 HP, once against a `srd_edition: '2014'` campaign's character (assert `hp_current === 0, is_stable === true`, no death-save entry) and once against a `'2024'` campaign's character (assert `hp_current === 1`, `is_stable` untouched/false, no Unconscious-via-0HP path taken) — a test that would fail if the nonlethal branch used one hardcoded edition's behavior for both.
- **Advance-turn prompt fires only for the correct participant, and only after their next-turn boundary** (§2.4/edge case 1): a character reduced to 0 HP mid-round → `advanceTurn` for the round's remaining OTHER participants must not flag `deathSaveDue`; only the `advanceTurn` call that lands specifically on the 0-HP character's own next turn must set it.
- **Monster instances default to instant death, opted-in instances use the full state machine** (edge case 12): a monster instance with `uses_death_saves = false` (default) reduced to 0 HP → `status === 'dead'` immediately, no death-save columns touched; a second instance with `uses_death_saves = true` reduced to 0 HP → behaves identically to the character-side test suite above (Unconscious, counters, stabilization, massive damage), proving the opt-in flag actually gates the whole machine rather than being read-and-ignored.
- **Row-locking / concurrency**: two near-simultaneous calls against the same 0-HP character — one a `death-save` roll landing on the 3rd success, one an `applyDamage` call landing a damage-at-0HP failure — must serialize (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`) so the outcome is deterministically one full ordering or the other (either "stabilized, so the damage call must then treat it as breaking Stable" or "failed first, so the death-save call sees an already-failed-out character and must reject"), never a lost-update where both succeed against a stale pre-transaction read.

## DM-configurable, never hardcoded

Named explicitly per this repo's persona convention:

- **Whether a monster falls unconscious and rolls death saves, or dies instantly at 0 HP** (§1.9) — the SRD itself frames this as a GM per-creature judgment call ("most GMs... mighty villains... common exceptions"), not a settled rule. Recommended mechanism: `monster_instances.uses_death_saves BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT false` (§2.2), a per-instance DM-editable flag — **not** a `campaigns`-wide toggle, since the SRD's own framing is per-creature, not per-table; if a future request needs a table-wide default, that would be a new, separate `campaigns.*` boolean following this app's existing single-purpose-column precedent (`allow_ability_reroll`), not overloaded onto this same instance-level flag.
- **Advantage/bonus-granting features on death saves** (§1.2) — the SRD explicitly leaves room for "spells and features that improve your chances" without enumerating them as a closed list; this app's own catalog of feats/class features/spells (not this rules doc's lane) is where any specific death-save-affecting feature would need to be modeled and read by the §2.3 roll endpoint — flagged as an extensibility point, not resolved here, and explicitly not something to hardcode a fixed bonus for in the base state machine.
- **Whether a hit fully absorbed by temporary HP at 0 HP still counts as a death-save failure** (§1.10, edge case 6) — genuinely unresolved by the grounding text, not DM-configurable in the sense of a named SRD option, but flagged here anyway since it's exactly the kind of ambiguity this project's own standard says shouldn't be silently resolved one way and presented as settled — the calling session should pick an explicit answer, document the choice (ideally back-referencing this section), and treat it as a project decision, not an SRD-confirmed rule either way.
